Overview

A robot's journey does not end at the doorway.

Service and delivery robots need to leave buildings, traverse outdoor spaces, enter a destination building, and find an object inside. I2O2I-Nav evaluates how these capabilities compose into a single, continuous mission.

The benchmark pairs reconstructed real-world environments with aligned 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) rendering and collision geometry. Its three-level evaluation progresses from independent navigation primitives to two-stage compositions and full indoor-to-outdoor-to-indoor missions. Each stage continues from its predecessor's actual endpoint.

From Real-World Scenes to Executable Missions

Real-world scans are processed into environments with visual reconstruction, collision geometry, navigation maps, and object- and area-level annotations. Building and floor labels define goals and boundary checks; evaluator annotations are withheld from navigation methods.

Connected Indoor–Outdoor Environments

The 20 composite scenes span schools, offices, residences, shopping areas, and parks, connecting multiple buildings and floors. Explore sampled views and reconstructed mesh geometry below.

MODULAR-Agent

Plan. Execute. Verify. Recover.

A vision-language model coordinates frozen navigation policies through a reusable skills library. State estimation informs workflow and subtask planning; execution feedback drives completion verification, stage handoffs, and bounded recovery.

Evaluation: Composition Remains Challenging

Using the same underlying policies, MODULAR-Agent raises I2O full success from 14.00% to 30.50% and O2I from 12.50% to 32.50%. Full four-stage success improves from 1.15% to 4.02%, while remaining low.

Evaluation uses a collision-constrained kinematic agent in Isaac Sim. All methods share task episodes, observations, action interfaces, success criteria, and execution budgets.

Metric definitions and policy assignments

SR: success rate. FSR: full success rate. CSR: mean fraction of stages completed as a valid ordered prefix. SPL: success-weighted path efficiency. TL: mean traveled distance in meters. RC: reference-route progress from the final position's planar projection. DCR: outdoor distance compliance rate. Ck: stage completion conditional on successful completion of all preceding stages.

L1 (O)/(S) denotes OmniNav/SocialNav executors; OutdoorNav (O) uses OmniNav-fast. L2/L3 (O + S) combines OmniNav for Exit, Entrance, and OVON with SocialNav for OutdoorNav. The fixed chain advances on executor completion proposals; MODULAR-Agent adds completion verification and bounded recovery. All table values except TL are percentages. Higher is better for these percentage metrics; TL is descriptive.

Where Do Missions Break Down?

Seeing outdoor space does not guarantee a valid exit; approaching a doorway does not guarantee entry. Visible objects can be missed, and repeated actions can leave a robot stuck. These cases motivate explicit completion verification and recovery.

Stage-conditional evaluation reveals both early attrition and final-goal failures. For MODULAR-Agent, conditional success at the final OVON stage is 21.21%, leaving efficient and reliable object search a substantial challenge.
